Print NEW base.stl, lid.stl and dpad.stl as a matched set.

DIMENSIONS (without USB plugs or end covers)
F body: 77.3 x 27.6 x 12.1 mm.
I body: 77.3 x 26.2 x 10.85 mm — 1.4 mm narrower, 1.25 mm thinner.
Overall height including unchanged round buttons: 12.45 mm (F: 13.15 mm).
Length retained for PCB, connector support and closure.
USB-A opening: 6.7 mm high, previously 8.3 mm from overlapping cutouts.
The new opening clears the exported model including rear attachment features;
it is not sized only to the 4.5 mm mating-shell dimension.

D-PAD RETENTION
The new pad has a central rounded heel resting on the PCB, as before, and a
larger spherical dome above it. A matching upper socket carried by the lid
locates that dome. This limits lateral motion and upward lift rather than
using a large loose skirt and the cross opening for location.
Nominal spherical surface clearance: 0.20 mm. The pad still needs running
clearance, so zero play is neither intended nor practical with an FDM bearing.
The four contact posts keep the original switch locations and 0.25 mm rest gap.
Dot faces USB-A. Do not rotate the pad to another orientation.
The pad is captured between the lid socket and PCB when the case is assembled;
it can fall out underneath the loose lid before the board is fitted.

PRINT
STLs are in mm, already oriented on the bed. Use 100% scale.
Base: flat floor down. Lid: outside face down. D-pad: thumb face down.
0.4 mm nozzle; 0.12 mm layers for lid/D-pad, 0.16 mm for base; 3 walls minimum.
Use the same rigid control material as the successful F buttons, not soft TPU
for the D-pad. Leave those existing buttons unchanged.
Inspect the slicer around the four small bearing braces and the central dome.
The lid's short inward braces may need carefully placed support; target their
undersides only. Keep support contacts away from the spherical bearing surface
and the two button bores. Check the D-pad dome overhang in preview as well.
Remove strings and support completely. Lightly deburr, but do not enlarge the
bearing or sand the spherical dome indiscriminately: that recreates the play.
Do not apply whole-part scale corrections to tune a single clearance.

ASSEMBLY
1. Test the D-pad in the loose lid and check the bearing is clean. It must rock
   freely when supported at its central heel. No screws, glue or metal axle.
2. Fit the microSD card before assembling. It is now entirely internal and the
   shell must be opened to remove it. The board/socket is lowered into the base
   from above; the old side opening is closed.
3. Fit the existing USB-C collar and board on their original seating datums.
4. DRY-FIT THE DISPLAY FLEX. The shallow floor leaves 2.4 mm below the PCB,
   versus the previous provisional 3.0 mm flex allowance. Actual folded flex
   thickness/bend and adhesive have not been measured. Arrange a gentle fold,
   verify there is clearance, and do not use the snaps to compress the cable.
   The electronics model clears the floor, but does not prove flex fit.
5. Insert your two F buttons and the new D-pad into the lid from underneath.
   Keep the D-pad's dot facing USB-A. Engage the USB-C hooks and lower the lid
   gently until the existing four snap positions engage.
6. Check all six controls with the diagnostic firmware before normal use.
   Each must register alone and release; check the untouched state after
   assembly. No control should be preloaded. If the bearing binds, stop and
   inspect rather than forcing the D-pad against the switches.
7. Fit the optional new TPU end covers after checking the bare case.

VALIDATION AND LIMITS
CAD: valid single solids, closed single-body STL meshes, pairwise fit,
278 exported PCB solids, original display envelope, board/socket insertion,
button travel to 0.6 mm, D-pad cardinal/diagonal tilt to +/-5 degrees,
positive D-pad lift/lateral retention, and sampled hooked lid closure.
Socket bearing clearance and actual switch force/return remain print tests.
Switch travel limits are not established by a rigid CAD clearance sweep.
The screen's actual installation and folded flex require the dry fit above.
F files are preserved. This is a revised physical-fit prototype, not a claim
that the new case has already been printed or mechanically qualified.
