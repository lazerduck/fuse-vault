FUSE VAULT CASE L — 22 October 2026
Full-case print trial for a Bambu P1S with its standard 0.4 mm nozzle.
Assumed material: ordinary PLA, matching revision K. These are starting settings,
not a physically qualified production profile.

PRINT
Print base.stl, lid.stl, dpad.stl, select.stl, back.stl, screen_shim.stl,
and TWO copies of universal_end_cap.stl. Reuse the existing USB-C collar;
a replacement usb_c_collar.stl is included. K round buttons are unchanged and may be reused if identified confidently.
Print the new L caps to get the stronger detents; old K caps will not gain retention.
Use the new L base, lid and D-pad together.

All STLs are millimetres, at 100% scale and already oriented on the bed.
Base and shim: underside down. Lid and buttons: outside/thumb face down.
Caps: closed end down, opening up. Collar: flange/end face down.
Do not auto-orient the controls or scale individual parts to adjust fit.

Suggested first print in Bambu Studio:
- Select P1S / 0.4 nozzle and your actual PLA filament and build plate.
- Use 0.12 mm layers for lid and controls; 0.16 mm for base, shim and caps.
- Four walls; five top/bottom layers. Use 100% infill for the D-pad and shim,
  and 20% infill for the shell/caps. Most thin features will already be solid.
- Keep the established filament temperature/flow profile. Use normal speed mode.
- Start with supports off; inspect the layer preview around the control flanges,
  shell snaps and cap stop ramps before printing. No support should fill guides.
- Keep normal elephant-foot compensation; do not add global XY scaling or hole
  compensation for this first trial. Remove first-layer burrs and strings only.
- Avoid placing a seam blob on the D-pad sides, button stems or snap beads.

The stock 0.4 mm nozzle is confirmed in Bambu's P1S quick-start specification:
https://cdn1.bambulab.com/documentation/quick-start-59b0cefdc0fc4/P1S/English%20version-Quick%20Start%20Guide%20for%20P1S.pdf
The settings above are our prototype starting values, not Bambu's case-specific advice.

WHAT CHANGED
D-pad contacts now follow the upper actuator surfaces in the PCB STEP model,
rather than the component placement origins. Corrections are about 0.35 mm:
SW5/SW7 toward negative Y, SW6 toward the screen, SW8 toward USB-A.
The contact noses are 2.2 mm diameter, with tapered shoulders, instead of K's
3.2 mm flat pads. The nominal rest height remains Z=3.65: no deliberate preload.
The central heel and orientation dot are retained. Low retaining-corner material
is relieved to allow the sampled +/-4-degree tilt while retaining the D-pad.
This may improve contact and available travel; it cannot change a switch's feel.

Screen: a 2.4 mm solid platform lifts the module off the PCB. Its shorter footprint
leaves the flex-exit end open. Screen nominal thickness is 1.5 mm per the supplied
drawing, not the obsolete 2.5 mm placeholder. With a 1.6 mm module and 0.2 mm TOTAL
adhesive allowance, the face is at Z=5.8; the relieved lid reserve reaches Z=5.95.
The bezel opening is flared for a less tunnel-like view. Do not add another shim
or thick foam tape: there is limited remaining clearance.

Pocket edges: outer-envelope vertical corners are rounded to 0.8 mm where they
intersect the shell; exposed top/bottom perimeter edges receive a 0.55 mm bevel.
The projecting SD shoulder corners receive a 0.5 mm bevel. Internal PCB locators,
USB support and the seam remain functional fit surfaces.

Closure: four existing ramped snap beads now have 0.35 mm nominal engagement,
up from 0.25 mm, with matching pockets. This gives a defined retention feature;
physical closing force and wear are still to be tried. Layer-line friction may
still contribute.

Cap retention: rounded bump radius is now 0.85 mm instead of 0.55 mm, with
matching 1.0 mm-radius case pockets instead of 0.70 mm. The pocket depth into
the case wall increases from 0.25 to 0.55 mm. Nominal sleeve-wall flex
is 0.40 mm per side instead of 0.10 mm. This is intentionally a more substantial
snap, using the broad sleeve walls to flex. Caps remain identical at either end
and in either 180-degree roll orientation. Fit and fatigue still need the print
trial; do not ram a reluctant cap against the connector.

ASSEMBLE
1. Before fitting the D-pad, press the four bare switches gently and compare them.
   If the screen-facing switch is already mushy, note that before judging the case.
2. Fit the SD card, PCB and USB-C collar into the base. Confirm the PCB is seated.
3. Dry-fit the shim on the clear screen area of the PCB: its long axis follows
   the display, with its short end stopping short of the flex exit/cutout.
   Assembly coordinates: X=19.1..44.5, Y=-18.0..-5.5, PCB top Z=1.6.
4. Place the display on the shim, aligned with the window, and check the cable
   route while gently lowering the lid. Do not tension, sharply crease or pinch
   the flex. The display must not carry lid-closing force.
5. Once aligned and clearance is confirmed, secure the platform and display with
   thin adhesive appropriate to your existing screen-mounting method. Allow no
   more than 0.2 mm total thickness across both adhesive interfaces. Remove old
   glue build-up from the stack; do not force the lid down onto the glass.
6. Insert Select, Back and the L D-pad from under the detached lid. The D-pad dot
   faces USB-A, away from the screen. The contacts are intentionally asymmetric;
   do not rotate the D-pad by 90 or 180 degrees.
7. Engage the USB-C hooks and lower the lid. Check all controls before snapping
   shut. Close each snap gently. A switch must not be held down at rest.
8. Test individual presses and releases in every direction, especially toward
   the screen. Then fit the two caps. Remove the caps before reopening the case.

REPORT AFTER THIS FULL PRINT
Does the screen-facing direction now click as cleanly as the others? Does every
press release? Is the screen readable without awkward angles? Does the shell
close without glass pressure and stay closed without excessive force? Are any
remaining corners uncomfortable in the hand or pocket?
A quick comparison with the bare switches helps distinguish case geometry from
switch hardware. No special test coupon is required for this iteration.

VALIDATION
See validation.json and detail-validation.json for actual results. CAD checks
cover solids, pairwise fit, source electronics, screen/SD reserves, control
insertion and movement, sampled shell closure, fixed lower switch housings,
source actuator contact centres, and cap removal. STLs are checked for watertight,
single-body meshes. These checks do not establish physical tactile feel, switch
compression/force, glue thickness, print accuracy or snap durability.
